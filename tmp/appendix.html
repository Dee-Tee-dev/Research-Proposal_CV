Appendix: Related Work

Datasets for socioeconomic visual diversity

Dollar Street pairs household photographs with country, region, and income metadata, making it possible to study visual variation that is absent from many web-image benchmarks [1]. Our work uses its public test split but constructs a locked, category-balanced subset: six household-object categories, four within-split income quartiles, and seven images in every category-quartile cell. This design controls category frequency while retaining the dataset's natural variation in country, scene, and object form.

Socioeconomic effects in vision-language models

Nwatu et al. [2] measured differences in CLIP image-text alignment and prompt-based retrieval across socioeconomic factors. We extend that question in three ways. First, we evaluate five model families rather than a single CLIP system. Second, we compare forced-choice classification, free-form captioning, and open-vocabulary detection on the same images. Third, we check whether automatic caption-term recall agrees with blinded human judgements, so a vocabulary mismatch is not treated as a model error without qualification.

Model families and task formulation

CLIP [3] provides the zero-shot contrastive baseline. BLIP [4] supplies image captioning and allows a fixed, training-free prompt comparison. Qwen2.5-VL [5] and InternVL3.5 [6] represent instruction-following multimodal models. YOLO-World [7] adds open-vocabulary detection. Unlike standard detection benchmarks, Dollar Street has no bounding-box annotations; we therefore report image-level correct-class detection and explicitly avoid mAP or IoU claims. The shared six-label setting lets us compare outputs while keeping prompts, images, and income grouping fixed.

Position of this study

The contribution is a small, reproducible benchmark rather than a claim about population-wide fairness. We report quartile gaps with category-stratified bootstrap intervals, retain negative and near-zero results, and separate term-based caption recall from human-rated semantic correctness. The resulting comparison shows that an apparent Q4 advantage for CLIP or caption recall does not automatically transfer to Qwen or InternVL classification, and that the measured conclusion depends on both task and evaluation rule.
